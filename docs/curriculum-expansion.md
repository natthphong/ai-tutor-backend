# Curriculum expansion

`internal/content/expansion.json` is an additive curriculum supplement generated by
`python3 scripts/expand_curriculum.py`. The generator does not read or rewrite
`internal/content/lessons.json`; the original 100 lesson IDs and records remain unchanged.

## Counts and numbering

The supplement contains 425 lessons, for a combined curriculum of 525 lessons:

- 300 practical lessons: 20 units x 5 lessons at each of A2, B1, and B2.
- 125 grammar lessons: 5 units x 5 lessons at each of Pre-A1, A1, A2, B1, and B2.
- Existing units remain 1-4. Practical units are 5-24 at A2-B2. Grammar units are
  5-9 at Pre-A1/A1 and 25-29 at A2-B2.
- Supplement ordinals are temporarily 101-525. Runtime ordering should use level,
  unit, and ordinal; ordinals do not replace the stable lesson IDs.

IDs are deterministic: `a2-practical-u05-l01` for practical lessons and
`grammar-prea1-u05-l01` for grammar lessons. Re-running the generator produces the
same JSON and runs static assertions for counts, unique IDs, ordinals, required
fields, vocabulary size, four drills, acceptance criteria, and source-unit types.

## Content model

Every lesson has a specific communication goal, pattern, original example, Thai
meaning and explanation, 2-4 vocabulary entries, pattern slots, four situational
drills, a conversation transfer task, and meaning-based acceptance criteria. Drill
targets are rubrics: equivalent natural answers are acceptable and exact sample
matching is not required.

Practical units progress from independent everyday communication to collaborative,
analytical, leadership, banking, risk, presentation, and negotiation work. The five
lessons in each unit perform different communication functions rather than repeating
one sentence frame with substituted nouns.

## Grammar source map

`source_units` records topic correspondence to unit numbers in Raymond Murphy,
*English Grammar in Use*, fifth edition. The reference was consulted selectively
from text extracted without layout. Its sequence is organized by grammar category
and is intended mainly for intermediate learners, so this curriculum reorders and
scaffolds topics by CEFR level instead of treating the book's order as a level scale.
An empty `source_units` list marks an original Pre-A1 foundation adaptation whose
topic is not covered by a matching numbered unit in this intermediate reference.
These declared exceptions cover basic forms of `be`, `this/that`, the negative of
`be`, and a simple imperative; the generator validates their IDs with an explicit
allowlist.

Coverage spirals through present, past, perfect, and future forms; questions and
negatives; articles and countability; prepositions; modals; comparisons;
conditionals; passives; relative clauses; reported speech; gerunds and infinitives;
and linking. B2 unit 25 synthesizes the conventional twelve tense forms. Future
perfect continuous is included only for recognition and the occasional need to
express duration up to a future point. The reference has no dedicated future
perfect continuous chapter; that small extension is original curriculum content,
while source unit 24 supports the neighboring future continuous and future perfect
contrast.

The source map is a navigation aid, not a reproduction. Explanations, examples,
Thai text, vocabulary, drills, prompts, and rubrics in the supplement are newly
authored. The map does not claim that the book assigns CEFR levels or covers the
app-specific practical contexts.
